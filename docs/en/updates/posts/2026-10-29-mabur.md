# Mabur — the Waybeam encoder, FEC and radio in one process, no RTP

In July 2026 developer **gilankpam** started [Mabur](https://github.com/gilankpam/mabur): an FPV video link for SigmaStar SSC338Q cameras where everything from sensor to screen is designed together. In four months the repository has collected more than a hundred documented bench runs and flights, and at the end of August it took in the [Waybeam](/en/software/waybeam-venc) encoder core.

Full overview, hardware requirements and installation: **[Mabur — an RTP-free video link](/en/software/mabur)**.

---

### 🔹 One process instead of three

The usual OpenIPC FPV setup is Waybeam (or Majestic) emitting RTP, plus WFB-ng packing and transmitting it, plus alink steering the encoder over HTTP. In Mabur a single daemon, `maburd`, does all of it:

- encodes H.265 with SVC-T temporal layers, using the Waybeam core moved in on August 29;
- applies FEC separately to each layer;
- injects the frames itself through `devourer`, a userspace RTL8812EU driver with no kernel module;
- takes link commands from the GS and changes the bitrate, ROI QP and IDR requests as plain function calls.

There is no RTP anywhere: the GS assembles whole frames and hands them to the player through a shared-memory ring.

---

### 🔹 What the pilot gets

- **UEP.** The base layer is protected more heavily than the droppable enhancement layer. Under loss smoothness drops, but the picture doesn't break up
- **Software NACK.** The GS asks for a resend of exactly the base-layer symbol FEC could not recover
- **Slice salvage.** Each frame has four slices, and an incomplete frame is shown with the gaps filled instead of disappearing
- **Automatic channel selection.** At startup the GS measures the channel set and moves the link to the cleanest one; under in-flight interference it changes channel within a fraction of a second
- **Phase lock.** The sensor rate is trimmed to the GS display's vblank, so latency is flat, with no beat
- **Up to 120 fps** on the IMX415 (1728×816), full view up to 90 fps
- **OSD** from the flight controller (MSP DisplayPort) plus a separate link-status bar, both burned into the DVR
- **Onboard SD recording** at a constant bitrate, driven by a button on the GS
- **Key pairing.** Commands are signed with SipHash, so a foreign GS cannot fly your drone

---

### 🔹 A ground station, even in a browser

The main GS is a **Radxa ZERO 3** with one or more RTL8812EU cards. There is also an **Orange Pi Zero 2W** port and a remote receive card built on a **TP-Link CPE510**.

The easiest way to look at the project is the [browser GS](https://gilankpam.github.io/mabur/). It is the same core compiled to WebAssembly, with the card over WebUSB in Chrome. By default the page runs as a **Spotter**: it only receives and displays, never transmits. That lets you watch a flight next to the main station.

---

### ⚠️ What to expect <Badge type="warning" text="EXPERIMENTAL" />

- **No releases.** Drone and GS firmware are built from the `openipc-builder` and `sbc-groundstations` forks
- **No cross-version compatibility, by design.** The drone and GS must run the same build, or there is no video and no control
- **SSC338Q + IMX415 only.** Maruko, CV610 and other sensors are not supported
- **No license** in the repository at the time of publication

For most pilots [Waybeam](/en/software/waybeam-venc) + [WFB-ng](/en/software/wfb-ng) remains the working setup. Mabur is worth a try if you want to see what an FPV link designed as a whole can look like, and you're ready to build the firmware yourself.
